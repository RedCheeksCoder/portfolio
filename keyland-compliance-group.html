Relevance

How this maps to Keyland Compliance Group

I haven't worked in transportation compliance before, and I won't pretend otherwise. What I have built repeatedly is the machinery a compliance-services business actually runs on: deadline-driven regulatory workflows, document intake, filing generation, and a CRM holding it all together.

Compliance tracking automation

Vanguard Credits tracks regulated dispute cases against statutory clocks — a 17-rule classification engine deciding what action each item needs, with scheduled 30- and 35-day follow-up rounds. Recurring filing deadlines like UCR, IFTA, MCS-150, and Clearinghouse queries are the same problem shape: a rule set, a calendar, and an escalation when something is about to lapse.

Document processing systems

That same platform parses four different credit-report formats and generates bureau-specific filing documents automatically. Genesis Credit added client-facing document-upload flows. Carrier document intake — CDLs, insurance certificates, MCS-150s — is the same pipeline: ingest a messy document, extract structured fields, generate the filing, attach it to the right record.

GoHighLevel — the CRM you named

Your posting lists GoHighLevel alongside HubSpot, Zoho, and Salesforce. I'm a GHL Certified Admin who has administered 360 sub-accounts and built production pipelines on it for six clients. If Keyland is already on GHL or considering it, that's zero ramp-up time.

A2P 10DLC — regulated messaging

I've secured A2P 10DLC approval for three separate clients. Any SMS programme reaching drivers or carrier contacts has to clear this, and it's a process that quietly blocks projects for months when nobody on the team has done it before.

Customer self-service

Sunwise's calculator and Meritex's AutoQuote both replace "fill in a form and wait for a callback" with an instant answer. Carriers checking authority status, requesting a permit, or getting a filing quote are the same interaction — and every one handled by software is one your team doesn't answer by phone.

Customer service automation

I built an AI chat agent for Genesis Credit on a dedicated knowledge base, plus an AI ticket-triage system that answers from the knowledge base when confident and routes to a human with a written summary when it isn't. Compliance questions are exactly the high-repetition, high-accuracy category this suits.

Payments & billing

Stripe flows handling transactions up to €7,450 for AEMR, and a Xendit-to-CRM integration for local payment methods that cards alone couldn't cover. Filing fees, consortium enrolment, and permit charges all need the same reliability.

Internal tools & reporting

Sunwise's lead-scored admin dashboard, Vanguard's Kanban board, and NextLevel's multi-view dashboard were all built so the operator sees priority order at a glance instead of reading a list. That's the same requirement as leadership reporting on carrier status and filing deadlines.

A note on how I work

I'm an Electronics Engineer by training and spent five-plus years as an engineering professor before moving into automation full-time. I still think in systems rather than tasks — which in practice means I map a process before I automate it, because automating a broken process just makes it break faster.

Everything above I built myself. That's the honest answer to "solo or team," and for a company at Keyland's stage I'd treat it as the relevant one: I can take something from problem statement to deployed and running without needing a team assembled first.
